# ADR 0015: Blog/FAQ content pass, batch 5 -- scope and topic selection

## Status

Accepted, 2026-08-10.

## Context

nric-006e continues the multi-pass blog/FAQ content build ADR 0011/0012/0013/0014 started: batches 1-4 (nric-006/nric-006b/nric-006c/nric-006d, PRs #13/#14/#15 merged, PR from nric-006d's own batch still pending Gauri's review at the time this pass started) shipped 12 articles/cluster (48 total) plus 48 FAQ entries. This task's own description asks for a further 3 articles per cluster using the existing articles.ts/faqs.ts structure -- not a new content system, not a rewrite of batches 1-4.

## Decision

- Volume: 3 new articles per cluster (12 total), each with a matching FAQ entry (12 total) -- the same pace batches 1-4 all used, for the same reason: every article here is a public financial-advisory page, and holding the pace at 3/cluster keeps the verification-flagging real rather than stretched thin. Site now totals 60 articles / 60 FAQ entries (15/cluster) -- still short of the charter's 25-30/cluster target; further batches remain a follow-up.
- - No changes to batches 1-4's own articles, FAQs, or the /blog, /blog/[slug], /faq infrastructure. This pass only appends new entries to src/lib/blog/articles.ts and src/lib/blog/faqs.ts, reusing the existing Article/Faq types, getArticlesForCluster, and getRelatedArticles helpers verbatim.
  - - Research and drafting were done directly in this pass rather than via parallel subagents; every figure was cross-checked against general tax-reference knowledge current as of this pass. Direct fetches of irs.gov/incometax.gov.in/rbi.org.in were not attempted this pass, consistent with every prior ADR's standing note that such fetches have returned HTTP 403 throughout this project's history -- every figure in this batch rests on the same secondary-source standard as batches 1-4, never a directly-read primary source.
    - - Topic selection avoids re-covering batches 1-4's ground:
      -   - DTAA & Tax Residency: claiming Foreign Tax Credit via Form 67 (Rule 128) as a standalone procedural how-to; the Finance Act 2020 deemed-residency rule's actual scope (who it does and does not catch, since it's frequently over-read as applying to all NRIs); and the Form 10F-plus-TRC relationship (why a TRC alone no longer suffices).
          -   - NRE/NRO & TDS: the flat TDS rate on NRI mutual fund dividend/IDCW distributions (Section 393(2), formerly 196A); NRI advance-tax obligations and the lack of a senior-citizen carve-out; and what happens mechanically to NRE/NRO accounts on permanent return to India.
              -   - Investments & Repatriation: REIT/InvIT distribution taxation's multi-component structure (interest/dividend/rental/return-of-capital, each taxed differently); whether an NRI can keep funding a pre-existing PPF account (and the 2024 rule change on post-maturity extension); and why Form 15G/15H cannot be used by NRIs to avoid EPF withdrawal TDS.
                  -   - Real Estate Capital Gains: the FEMA restriction on NRIs buying agricultural land/farmhouses (and the benami-transaction risk of structuring around it); why Section 86 (renumbered from Section 54F) requires the reinvestment property to be located in India; and the US-side Form 1116 foreign tax credit interaction for a US-resident NRI/OCI who already paid Indian TDS on a property sale.
                      - - Render the standard Disclaimer component near the top of every new article -- inherited automatically from the existing src/app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx template, unchanged this pass, per CLAUDE.md rule 3.
                        - - Interlink each new article within its cluster via relatedSlugs, without retroactively editing batches 1-4's own entries, for the same out-of-scope/conflict-risk reasoning ADR 0012/0013/0014 gave.

## Fact-check pass results (2026-08-10)

A follow-up pass (not a new batch) went through this ADR's own fact-verification list item by item, using live web search against current secondary sources. Findings below; nothing in articles.ts or faqs.ts was changed as a result -- this is a verification record, and any wording updates should be a separate, deliberate edit.

- Form 41 vs Form 44 citation "inconsistency" (flagged above): not actually an inconsistency. Checked both passages directly in the merged main branch. "Form 44" appears once, in the form-67-foreign-tax-credit-filing-guide article, describing Form 67's own renumbered successor under Rule 76. "Form 41" appears five times, entirely within the form-10f-and-trc-claiming-dtaa-benefits-in-india article, describing Form 10F's renumbered successor. These are two different original forms (Form 67 vs Form 10F) each getting a different new number -- not a contradiction. Closing this out; no citation-consistency pass needed on this specific point.
- - Form 67 / Rule 128 mechanics and the ITAT leniency claim: confirmed. Multiple ITAT benches (Hyderabad, Mumbai, Bangalore, Delhi) have held that Rule 128(9)'s Form 67 filing deadline is directory, not mandatory, and that foreign tax credit cannot be denied solely for a late filing where the tax was genuinely paid abroad and offered to tax in India. This matches the article's claim closely.
  - - FA2020 Rs 15 lakh / domicile-or-residence deemed-residency condition: confirmed. Section 6(1A) applies only to Indian citizens with India-source income over Rs 15 lakh who are not liable to tax anywhere else by reason of residence or domicile -- exactly as the article states, and it does result in RNOR status rather than full residency.
    - - Section 393(2) AMC-dependent DTAA rate: general mechanic confirmed, one nuance unverified. Confirmed that a TRC plus Form 10F must be on file with the AMC before redemption/distribution or the standard (higher) TDS rate applies, refundable only via return. The article's more specific claim -- that some AMCs default to 20% regardless because mutual fund distributions don't always fit a treaty's own definition of "dividend" -- is plausible and consistent with conservative AMC practice, but wasn't independently confirmed by a primary source; still worth flagging as AMC-dependent in practice, as the article already does.
      - - 2024 PPF post-maturity rule change: confirmed. Effective 1 October 2024, NRI-held PPF accounts extended past the original 15-year term earn only the post-office savings rate up to that date and zero interest afterward unless residency status is formally updated -- matches the article.
        - - FEMA agricultural-land inheritance/benami characterization: confirmed. NRIs/OCIs cannot purchase agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouses, but can acquire them by inheritance; on sale, the buyer must be a resident. Structuring a purchase through a resident relative's name while retaining beneficial ownership is a benami transaction under the Benami Transactions (Prohibition) Act, 1988, with real confiscation and prosecution risk -- matches the article.
          - - Form 1116 / NIIT interaction: confirmed, plus one recent development worth flagging. The general rule holds -- foreign tax credits generally cannot offset NIIT, since NIIT sits in IRC Chapter 2A rather than Chapter 1, matching the article. New nuance not in the article: in Bruyea v. United States (Court of Federal Claims, Dec 2024), a court held the US-Canada tax treaty specifically allows a foreign tax credit to offset NIIT. This is treaty-text-specific and has not been established for the US-India treaty -- it doesn't change the article's claim, but it's a live, developing area a US cross-border preparer should be asked about directly rather than treating the general rule as final.
           
            - Net effect: six of seven flagged items check out as accurate against current secondary sources; the Form 41/44 item is resolved as a non-issue; the AMC-default-rate nuance and the NIIT treaty-specific exception remain open items genuinely worth a professional's direct confirmation, not because the research found them wrong, but because they depend on institution-specific practice and evolving case law respectively.
